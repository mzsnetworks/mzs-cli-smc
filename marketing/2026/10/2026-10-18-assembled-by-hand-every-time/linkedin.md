# LinkedIn — assembled-by-hand-every-time


Every quarter somebody asks the network team a question that takes two days to answer and gets asked again in ninety days.

How many sites are on the supported image. Which circuits are under contract and when each renews. What changed in the environment during the period the auditor is asking about. How many devices are past their support date.

None of these are hard questions. They are assembly jobs. The answer exists, spread across an inventory system that is partly accurate, a monitoring platform, a folder of contracts, and two engineers' memory.

So an engineer builds the spreadsheet. It is correct on the day it is delivered, never updated, and ninety days later the same engineer builds it again because nobody trusts the old one. That is the actual cost, and it never appears in a budget, because it is spread across four afternoons a year.

The second cost is quieter. Because the answer is expensive, it only gets asked for when something forces it. Nobody asks casually, so the estate is examined on the schedule of external deadlines rather than when a decision needs it.

ITOC Dashboard exists for this. Not another alerting console, which you already have, but operational state aggregated in one place: inventory, image versions, configuration posture, change history and lifecycle dates, in a view somebody outside the network team can open without asking an engineer first.

The test is simple. Someone in finance, compliance or leadership has a question about the network. Can they get an accurate answer themselves, this afternoon?

A question that costs two days to answer is a question your organization stops asking.
