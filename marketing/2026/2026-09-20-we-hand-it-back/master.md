The quiet objection to bringing in an automation partner is not "will it work." It is "will we be able to touch it afterwards."

That worry is earned. Plenty of consulting engagements leave behind something that runs, that nobody in-house fully understands, and that quietly converts into a retainer nobody chose.

We design against it, and it shows up in unglamorous places. Automation lives in your repository, not ours. Tooling is Python, Ansible, Terraform — things you can hire for. Anything non-obvious is written down as reasoning, not just as configuration, because the next engineer needs to know why the exception exists. And handover includes someone on your team running the thing in front of us, not a walkthrough.

The test we use: if we stopped tomorrow, could your team make a routine change to what we built, safely, without calling us? If the honest answer is no, it is not finished.

You can test for this before signing anything. Ask what handover looks like, and listen for whether the answer contains a date and a named person on your side who will be operating it. "Documentation and a walkthrough" is not a handover. It is a delivery with a paragraph attached.

Some clients keep us on afterwards. That is a decision worth making because the work is valuable, not because the exit is expensive.
