A network that doubled its sites in three years and kept the same team did not get more efficient. It got later.

Manual change scales linearly. Thirty sites take roughly three times what ten sites take, and the arithmetic does not care how good the engineers are. So the backlog grows, the standards drift because standards are enforced by attention, and the work that gets deferred is always the invisible kind — the upgrade, the cleanup, the documentation.

The usual response is a hiring request. Sometimes that is right. Often it is an automation decision being deferred by a year, at the cost of a salary, with the added problem that the new hire inherits the same manual process and reaches the same ceiling.

The question worth asking before the requisition: of the work consuming the team, how much is judgment and how much is execution? Judgment needs people. Execution — provisioning, compliance checks, policy updates, upgrades — needs a tested, reviewable, rollback-able mechanism.

We have seen both answers. Some teams genuinely need another engineer. More often they need the same engineers not doing the same task for the fortieth time.

If you are writing that job description now, it is worth an hour with someone who will tell you which one it is.
