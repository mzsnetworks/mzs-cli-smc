# LinkedIn — first-thirty-days


The most common objection we hear is not about price. It is "we tried this before and it went nowhere."

That is a fair thing to be worried about, so here is the shape of a first month, concretely.

Week one is reading, not building. Current configurations, the actual change process as practiced rather than as documented, what the team already automated and why it stopped being used. There is almost always something half-built. It usually failed for an organizational reason, not a technical one, and that reason is still there.

Week two is picking one process. Not the most impressive — the most repeated. It has to be something the team does often enough that they feel the difference within the month.

Weeks three and four are building it end to end and handing it over: tested against a model, peer-reviewed, logged, reversible, and runnable by somebody other than whoever wrote it. That last one is the difference between automation and a personal script.

What you have at the end of thirty days is one process that no longer consumes a person, and enough evidence to decide whether the next one is worth doing.

Thirty days is not enough to change how a network is run. It is enough to find out whether we are the people who should.
