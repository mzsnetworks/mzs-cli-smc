# LinkedIn Platform Rules

Layers on top of `SHARED.md`.

## Format

- **Length:** long-form allowed, but bounded. **Draft to 1,600–1,850 characters (~260–320 words); the checker fails below 1,300 or above 1,900.** LinkedIn's own hard cap is ~3,000 — that is the platform's limit, not ours, and writing toward it is how seventeen published renders ended up at 2,001–2,090. Don't pad; go as long as the idea earns, and stop at 1,850.
- **The fold:** LinkedIn truncates at ~210 characters (≈3 lines) behind "…see more." The hook *and* a reason to expand must live above that fold.
- **Structure:** hook line → POV → cited data → judgment → close → hashtags → sources block.
- **Paragraphs:** short. One to three lines. White space carries long-form on LinkedIn.

## Voice

- Professional but personal. First-person reflection works here ("Here's what I've concluded after building in this space").
- Story + data + takeaway is the default shape.
- The close can be a question — a real one that invites discussion, not "Agree? Comment below."

## Emoji

- Near-zero. LinkedIn's professional context makes emoji read as junior. The reference LinkedIn post uses none. Default to none; one is the ceiling.

## Hashtags

- 2–3, technical, at the very end. PascalCase. Specific to the post's subject (`#NetDevOps #NetworkAutomation`), never generic (`#tech #leadership`).

## Sources

- Include a `## Sources` block at the bottom with the real links behind every stat cited. This is a credibility signal and a Factcheck requirement.

## Calibration

The reference `content/2026/2026-06-24-ai-makes-us-judges/linkedin.md` is the target **for shape and voice**: a decade-of-experience hook, a NetOps/NetDevOps reframe, three cited Gartner-class data points, a judgment section on AI-and-judgment, a triad landing, one closing question, two hashtags, a sources block.

**It is not the target for length.** That post is 2,831 characters — written before the bands above existed, and it fails the current checker. Copy its structure, not its size. This is the contradiction that made the prose limits ineffective for months: the rule said one number and twenty-four in-context examples showed another, and the examples won.
