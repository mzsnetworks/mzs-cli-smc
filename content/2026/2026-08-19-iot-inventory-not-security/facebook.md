# Facebook — iot-inventory-not-security

Your IoT problem isn't security. It's inventory.

The budget points at segmentation, NAC, firewalls. Then someone asks what's actually on the network, and the room goes quiet.

We've walked into buildings where one flat VLAN carried hundreds of devices nobody could name. IoT never arrives through the network team: facilities buys the cameras, clinical buys the pumps, a pilot ends and the devices stay powered on for years.

A policy needs a subject. "Least privilege for IoT" isn't a policy until you can say which devices, doing what, talking to whom.

The order that works is boring: enumerate, identify, classify by behavior, then policy per class.

You can't write policy for a device you can't name. Count first. Segment second.

How many devices are on your network right now — within ten?
