# Instagram — definition-of-normal


Anomaly detection needs a definition of normal. 📉 Most networks have never had a quiet enough week to learn one.

That's the part the demo skips.

→ The interface that resets every few days → normal
→ The link that saturates every afternoon → normal
→ The device that drops SNMP during backups → normal

⚠️ None of it was labeled as broken. The model can't tell "how the network behaves" from "what nobody has fixed yet."

So you get a system that's quietest about your oldest problems. 🔇

It'll catch a new fault tomorrow and stay silent on the chronic one costing you hours every month — because by its only definition, that's the baseline.

Clean what you already know about first.

What has your monitoring stopped alerting on because it happens every week?

## Carousel slide ideas

1. **Cover** — "Anomaly detection needs a definition of *normal*."
2. **The problem** — Most networks have never had a quiet enough week to learn one.
3. **What the demo shows** — Clean baseline. Injected fault. Everyone impressed.
4. **What production has** — Three years of running slightly wrong.
5. **What it learns** — The interface that resets. The link that saturates. The device that drops SNMP during backups.
6. **Why** — None of it was labeled as broken. It's all in the training data.
7. **The result** — A system that is *quietest about your oldest problems*.
8. **Not an argument against the tools** — An argument about sequencing.
9. **Do this first** — Fix or explicitly accept what you already know about. Label the known-bad periods. Start where normal is stable.
10. **CTA** — "It tells you what *changed*. Not what was wrong all along."
