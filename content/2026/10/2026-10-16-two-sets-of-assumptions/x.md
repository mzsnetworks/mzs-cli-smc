## Thread

1/ An acquisition doesn't join two networks.

It joins two sets of assumptions about who is trusted — and nobody documented either one. 🧵

2/ The technical work gets scoped first, because it's the part you can see.

Overlapping address space. Routing between two cores. DNS that has to resolve both ways.

That work is real. It's also the easy half.

3/ The half that overruns is the part nobody wrote down.

4/ Every network encodes a trust model in its access lists, its firewall zones, and the things it never bothered to restrict.

5/ Somewhere in the acquired estate: a flat segment carrying production traffic, because ten years ago the team was small and everyone was in one building.

Somewhere in yours: a rule permitting a source range for a project that ended in 2021.

6/ Neither is written down as a policy.

Both are policies.

Connect the two and you've merged the permissions without merging the reasoning.

7/ What you get is not the union of two security postures. It's the weaker of the two, applied to everything.

So the first question isn't how the routing works. It's which assumptions are we inheriting — and who can still explain them?
