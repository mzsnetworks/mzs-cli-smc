# Instagram — the-change-you-didnt-make


The change you didn't make is also a decision. 🗂️

It just never enters the record.

Change management captures things that happen. Someone proposes, reviews, approves, logs it. Deciding to leave something alone produces none of that.

No ticket. No approver. No artifact.

The deferrals are invisible to every process we have. ⚠️

This isn't recklessness. Most are good calls at the time:

→ Not upgrading that firmware this quarter — the window is full, the risk is low
→ Not replacing that switch yet — the budget is next year
→ Not enabling that feature until someone can test it properly

The problem is what happens after. A change that goes ahead gets reviewed and revisited when it misbehaves. A change that doesn't gets discussed once, in a meeting, then stops existing.

The reasoning evaporates. The condition it left behind does not.

Two years later somebody inherits that firmware version with no idea it was a choice. It reads as neglect. Nobody reviews it — from outside, there's nothing to review. ⚙️

What helps is small: write down what you're not doing, why, and what would change your mind.

That last part does the work. "We revisit if the vendor issues an advisory" is a trigger, and a trigger can fire. "Not now" cannot.
