# Instagram — latency-in-the-humans


Half of this job is waiting, and none of our planning accounts for it. ⏳

TAC callback. Maintenance window. RMA in transit. The change board that meets Thursday. Access approval from someone on leave.

We estimate the technical work carefully. Four hours. And we're usually right.

Then it takes three weeks — and everyone calls that an estimating failure, when it was an estimate of the wrong quantity. 📉

The four hours was accurate. Nobody counted the six handoffs, each with a queue in front of it.

The queue isn't a rounding error. It's most of the duration.

Three things help, and none is working faster:

→ Name the waits in the estimate. "Four hours of work across two weeks, because of the change board and the RMA." Honest, and it makes the queue visible to whoever can shorten it.

→ Start the clock early on anything with a queue. Raise the access request, open the case, lower the TTL, order the spare. Same wait, different position.

→ Run the waits in parallel. Most plans serialize them by accident.

And waiting isn't idle. It's where you write the rollback, stage the config, brief the next shift. ⚙️

The work is fast. The queue is not. Estimate the queue.

What's the longest you've waited on something that took ten minutes to fix?
