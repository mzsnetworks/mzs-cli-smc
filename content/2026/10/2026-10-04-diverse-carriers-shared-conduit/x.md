## Thread

1/ Which of your circuits have genuinely diverse paths?

Not diverse carriers. Diverse conduit.

Those are different purchases, and only one of them is on the contract. 🧵

2/ Carrier diversity is commercial. Two vendors, two bills, two support numbers.

Path diversity is physical. It's about where the fiber is actually buried.

You can have the first without the second.

3/ The reason is mundane: carriers lease capacity from each other.

A regional provider sells you a circuit that rides another carrier's fiber for the middle third of its route.

4/ So both of your "diverse" links leave the building on the same side, cross the same bridge, and pass through the same manhole two miles away.

Nobody lied. Nobody at either carrier had the whole picture either.

5/ You find out on the day a backhoe goes through that manhole.

Both circuits drop inside the same second. The failover you tested carefully has nowhere to fail over to.

6/ The fix isn't more redundancy. It's asking for the route.

• Request the KMZ or circuit layout from both carriers
• Compare them
• A refusal is itself information

7/ Where the routes converge, you have a real choice: accept the risk knowingly, buy a genuinely diverse path, or put a different medium on the backup.

Fixed wireless shares no conduit with anything.

Redundancy you haven't traced is two invoices.
