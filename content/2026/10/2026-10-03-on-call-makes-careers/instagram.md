# Instagram — on-call-makes-careers

On-call is where careers are actually made, and almost nobody says so out loud. 🌙

We describe it as a tax.

Something you endure, negotiate down, or escape by getting senior enough.

All of that is fair. The sleep cost is real. The toll on people is real. A rotation that's genuinely brutal should be fixed, not romanticized. ⚙️

But it's also the single fastest place engineering judgment gets built.

Both things are true. We only ever say the first one.

Here's why it works, when it works.

📍 You get the whole system, not your slice

Day to day you're inside your area. At 2am you're handed something spanning four of them.

You reason across the boundaries, quickly, with the actual dependencies — not the diagram of them.

Nothing else in the job teaches the shape of an environment that fast.

📍 You learn what actually breaks

As opposed to what's supposed to.

Every environment has a gap between documented design and real behavior. On-call is where you meet it, repeatedly, with consequences.

📍 You learn to act on incomplete information

Under time pressure, then live with the result.

Decide. Watch. Be wrong sometimes. Adjust.

That loop is what separates engineers who have opinions from engineers whose opinions are worth something.

The catch: none of it is automatic.

A rotation only teaches if the pages are real, the runbooks exist, somebody senior is reachable without shame, and last month's fixes actually got made.

A rotation where the same alert fires nightly and nothing is ever fixed doesn't build judgment. It builds resentment — and then it builds a resignation.

If you run one: the difference between a rotation that develops people and one that burns them is whether the work it surfaces gets funded. That's a management decision, not an engineering one.

And if you're on one right now, at an hour you didn't choose — you're not just covering. You're building the thing that makes you hard to replace.

What did on-call teach you that nothing else would have? 👇
