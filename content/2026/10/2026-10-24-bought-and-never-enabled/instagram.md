# Instagram — bought-and-never-enabled


Every environment I inherit has a feature that was bought, licensed, renewed for years — and never turned on. 💳

It's usually something good. Segmentation. Posture checking. The analytics module. The redundancy option on a platform that only ever ran standalone.

Somebody made a sound case. The money was approved. The project slipped. The person who understood it left.

It isn't simply waste. Waste would be cheaper.

→ It costs the license every year, and nobody cancels it — because canceling means someone has to state on the record that it won't be used.

→ It shapes the architecture on paper. The diagram shows the capability, and decisions get made next to it as though it were there.

→ It gets assumed to be working by everyone who wasn't in the room when it was deferred. ⚠️

That last one is the dangerous part. The auditor sees the license. The security review sees the product name. Nobody sees the checkbox — it's three menus deep and nobody has a reason to look.

Somewhere in most estates is a control that appears in a compliance document and not in the running configuration.

The fix isn't a project. It's a sentence: the date it gets enabled and who owns that, or the date the contract ends. ⚙️

Either is a decision. What we usually have is a renewal, made by nobody, every year, forever.
