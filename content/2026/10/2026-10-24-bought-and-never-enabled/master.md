# Master — bought-and-never-enabled

**Preset:** Professional (LinkedIn personal profile + Instagram @mzsnetworks)
**Date:** 2026-10-24
**Thesis:** The feature you bought and never enabled is technical debt with a maintenance contract. It costs money every year, shapes the architecture on paper, and is quietly assumed to be working by everyone who was not in the room when it was deferred.
**Pillar:** Brownfield Engineering
**Source:** ideas-2026-08-27 #30
**Stats:** none. Judgment and practice only; no statistic stated as fact.

---

Every environment I inherit has at least one feature that was bought, licensed, renewed for years, and never turned on.

It is usually something good. Segmentation. Posture checking. The analytics module. The redundancy option on a platform that only ever ran standalone. Somebody made a sound case for it, the money was approved, and then the project that would have enabled it slipped, and then the person who understood it left.

It is not simply waste. Waste would be cheaper.

It costs the license every year, and nobody cancels it, because canceling requires someone to state on the record that it will not be used.

It shapes the architecture on paper. The diagram shows the capability. Decisions get made next to it, as though it were there.

And it gets assumed to be working by everyone who was not in the room when it was deferred. That is the dangerous part. The auditor sees the license. The security review sees the product name. Nobody sees the checkbox, because the checkbox is three menus deep and nobody has a reason to look.

Somewhere in most estates there is a control that appears in a compliance document and does not appear in the running configuration.

The fix is not a project, it is a sentence. For each of these, write down one of two things: the date it gets enabled and who owns that, or the date the contract ends and that it will not be renewed.

Either is a decision. What we usually have instead is a renewal, made by nobody, every year, forever.

If you want an unpleasant hour, pull your license list and tick off which of them you could demonstrate actually running today.
