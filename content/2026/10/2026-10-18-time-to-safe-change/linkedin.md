# LinkedIn — time-to-safe-change


Automation coverage is the wrong metric.

It is the one every program reports, because it is the one you can compute. Sixty percent of provisioning automated. Forty percent of compliance checks. The number goes up every quarter and nobody can say whether anything got better.

Here is the problem with it. Coverage measures how much you built. It says nothing about whether change got safer, or faster, or less dependent on one person being awake.

You can automate sixty percent of a process and still have every change wait on the same manual approval. You can hit ninety percent coverage on the tasks that were never the bottleneck. Coverage rewards building, and building is not the point.

The metric that holds up is time-to-safe-change: from "we have decided to make this change" to "it is live, verified, and revertible." Measured end to end, including the waiting.

It works because it is honest about the whole path. If the script runs in four seconds but the change advisory board meets on Thursdays, your time-to-safe-change is a week — and that is the truth about your operation regardless of what the coverage dashboard says.

It also survives a conversation with someone who does not work in networking. A CFO cannot interpret "sixty percent coverage." They can interpret "a firewall policy change used to take nine days and now takes one." That is the same sentence that justifies the next phase of investment.

Measure it once before you automate anything, so the number means something later. Then measure it per change type, because the average across all changes hides the one that still takes three weeks.

Coverage tells you how much you built. Time-to-safe-change tells you whether it mattered.

Which one is on your dashboard?
