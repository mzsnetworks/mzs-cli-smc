# Instagram — time-to-safe-change


Automation coverage is the wrong metric. 📊

It's the one every program reports, because it's the one you can compute.

→ 60% of provisioning automated
→ 40% of compliance checks
→ The number climbs every quarter
→ Nobody can say whether anything got better

⚙️ Coverage measures how much you built. Not whether change got safer.

The metric that holds up: time-to-safe-change. From "we decided" to "it's live, verified, and revertible" — including the waiting. ⏱️

If the script runs in four seconds but the advisory board meets Thursdays, your number is a week. That's the truth about your operation.

A CFO can't interpret "60% coverage." They can interpret "nine days down to one."

Which one is on your dashboard?

## Carousel slide ideas

1. **Cover** — "Automation coverage is the *wrong metric*."
2. **Why everyone reports it** — Because it's the one you can compute.
3. **What it measures** — How much you built.
4. **What it doesn't** — Whether change got safer, faster, or less dependent on one person being awake.
5. **The trap** — 90% coverage on the tasks that were never the bottleneck.
6. **The metric that holds** — Time-to-safe-change: decided → live, verified, revertible.
7. **Including the waiting** — Script runs in 4 seconds. Board meets Thursdays. Your number is a week.
8. **Why it travels** — A CFO can't read "60% coverage." They can read "nine days to one."
9. **How to use it** — Measure before you automate. Then measure per change type — the average hides the one that takes three weeks.
10. **CTA** — "Coverage says how much you built. This says whether it *mattered*."
