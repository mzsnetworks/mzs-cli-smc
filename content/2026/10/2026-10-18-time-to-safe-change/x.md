## Thread

1/ Automation coverage is the wrong metric.

It's the one every program reports, because it's the one you can compute. 🧵

2/ Sixty percent of provisioning automated. Forty percent of compliance checks.

The number goes up every quarter and nobody can say whether anything actually got better.

3/ Coverage measures how much you built.

It says nothing about whether change got safer, or faster, or less dependent on one person being awake.

4/ You can automate 60% of a process and still have every change wait on the same manual approval.

You can hit 90% coverage on the tasks that were never the bottleneck.

5/ The metric that holds up is time-to-safe-change:

From "we have decided to make this change" to "it is live, verified, and revertible."

End to end. Including the waiting.

6/ If the script runs in four seconds but the change advisory board meets on Thursdays, your time-to-safe-change is a week.

That's the truth about your operation, whatever the coverage dashboard says.

7/ It also survives a conversation with a CFO.

They can't interpret "sixty percent coverage." They can interpret "a firewall policy change used to take nine days and now takes one."

That's the sentence that funds the next phase.
