Spare parts are a strategy, not a storeroom. 📦

The time it takes to replace a failed device is a design constraint nobody writes down.

Every resilient design assumes something about it. A pair tolerates one failing, and the design is sound for as long as the second holds.

How long is that? Until the first is replaced. ⚠️

A logistics question — answered years earlier by whoever signed the support contract, not by anyone in the design review.

The design says "redundant." The contract says:

→ Next business day
→ To a dock staffed weekdays
→ For a site whose nearest engineer is three hours away

Both are statements about the same risk. They've never been in the same document.

You aren't running without redundancy for four hours. You're running without it for most of a week. ⚙️

What people get wrong is treating this as procurement. Sometimes it is. Often the opposite: accept the slow replacement and design for it, because a four-hour contract on a site that tolerates a long run buys down a risk you didn't have.

Either way the decision needs the number. Not the contract tier — the elapsed time from "it died" to "it's running again."

Redundancy isn't a property of the hardware. It's a property of how fast you can get the other one back.

What is your actual replacement time, and who knows it?
