# LinkedIn — the-change-you-didnt-make


The change you did not make is also a decision. It just never enters the record.

Change management captures things that happen. Someone proposes, someone reviews, someone approves, it gets logged. Deciding to leave something alone produces none of that. No ticket, no approver, no artifact.

Which means the deferrals are invisible to every process we have.

I do not mean recklessness. Most are good calls at the time. We are not upgrading that firmware this quarter, because the window is full and the risk is low. We are not replacing that switch yet, because the budget is next year. We are not enabling that feature until someone can test it properly. Every one defensible, and I have made all three.

The problem is what happens afterward. A change that goes ahead gets reviewed and revisited when it misbehaves. A change that does not gets discussed once, in a meeting, and then stops existing. The reasoning evaporates. The condition it left behind does not.

Two years later somebody inherits that firmware version with no idea it was a choice. It reads as neglect, or as how things have always been. Nobody reviews it, because from outside there is nothing to review.

What helps is small. When you decide against something that mattered enough to discuss, write it down where the work lives: what you are not doing, why, and what would change your mind. That last part does the work — "we revisit if the vendor issues a security advisory" is a trigger, and a trigger can fire. "Not now" cannot.

The worst inherited environments are not the ones where people made bad decisions. They are the ones where nobody can tell which conditions were decided at all.

What did your team decide not to do this quarter?
