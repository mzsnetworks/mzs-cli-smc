## Thread (alternate)

1/ An SLA is not a design. Four nines on the contract, one fiber path in the ground, one power feed at the demarc. 🧵

2/ We've walked sites where the paperwork promised 99.99% and the reality was a single entrance facility and two "diverse" carriers whose fiber shared one conduit under one bridge.

3/ Diverse on the invoice. Adjacent in the dirt.

4/ None of it is fraud. Nobody asked the physical question, because the contract had already answered a different one.

5/ The distinction that matters: an SLA transfers risk. It does not reduce it.

6/ When the fiber is cut, a credit arrives eventually — usually a fraction of one month's bill. It doesn't run payroll. It compensates you for the outage; it was never built to prevent one.

7/ The useful questions aren't in the contract:
• where do the paths physically diverge — on a map?
• whose conduit is that?
• real restoration time in that metro?
• when primary is down, what actually runs?

8/ An SLA tells you what you're owed when it breaks. A design tells you whether it breaks. #NetOps #NetworkDesign
