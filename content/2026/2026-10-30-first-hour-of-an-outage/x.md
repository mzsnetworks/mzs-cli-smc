## Thread

1/ The most expensive hour of an outage is the first one.

It's rarely spent diagnosing. It's spent deciding whose problem this is. 🧵

2/ The pattern is always the same. The bridge fills up — fourteen people, most of whom don't need to be there.

3/ In sequence, each team demonstrates the fault isn't theirs.

Network: clean interfaces, stable adjacencies.
App team: the service responds on localhost.
Storage: green.

4/ Every one of those demonstrations is honest, competent, and takes about twelve minutes.

At the end of the hour everybody is correct and nothing is fixed.

5/ That isn't defensiveness. It's structural.

Nobody can assign the problem, so everybody defends against being assigned it. That's rational on a bridge with no authority on it.

6/ The fix separates two questions that get collapsed into one:

Who owns the problem — a role, assigned at minute zero, before anyone knows the cause.
Who caused it — a finding, and it belongs in a postmortem.

7/ The owner doesn't need to be the deepest expert in the room.

Arguably shouldn't be — the expert needs their attention on the fault.

8/ The first hour isn't where you find the cause.

It's where you decide whether the next three have an owner.
