# Instagram — status-update-stops-calls

The fastest way to stop the phone ringing during an incident is four lines of text. 📟

Here's the loop I've watched too many times:

→ the technical work competes with the communication work
→ communication loses, because it isn't fixing anything
→ updates stop
→ people start calling to find out what's happening
→ the technical work gets slower

The people calling you aren't impatient. ⚙️

They're uninformed, and calling is the only lever they have.

Four lines, sent on a clock:

1️⃣ What's broken — in their language, not the device's. "Users at Denver can't reach the ERP," not "core-sw02 is flapping."

2️⃣ Who's affected — the scope you've actually confirmed. Still confirming? Say that.

3️⃣ What we're doing right now — one sentence, present tense. Not the plan for the next three hours.

4️⃣ When you'll hear next — an actual clock time.

That last one is the line that stops the calls. It's the only line that does.

What stays out:

→ speculation about cause
→ an ETA you can't support
→ anything you'll have to retract

An unproven cause isn't analysis during an incident. It's a rumor with your name on it, and it will outrun the correction.

Line 4 is the hard one, because it commits you.

Send it anyway. Then send the next one on time even if it says nothing changed.

An on-time "no change" buys more trust than an early guess. Every time.

None of this makes the outage shorter. It makes it quieter — which is often the same thing.

Four lines. On a clock. With no guesses in them.

What's in your updates that doesn't need to be? 👇
