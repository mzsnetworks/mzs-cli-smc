Have you ever been unable to say "I don't know yet" during an incident?

I have. Not because anyone forbade it. Because of who was on the call, and because forty minutes in, saying it out loud felt like admitting I had wasted forty minutes.

So I said something else. Something shaped like progress. "We're seeing some irregularity on the WAN side, still narrowing it down." All true, all useless, and it bought me fifteen more minutes of not being asked anything.

What that sentence actually did was cost the organization fifteen minutes.

Because the people listening make decisions off what you say. "Still narrowing it down" means hold, stay on the call, do not start the workaround. "I don't know yet, and I won't for at least an hour" means something completely different. It means start the manual process, tell the client, call in the second team. Those are useful decisions, and they were available the whole time.

The admission is not the expensive part. The vagueness is.

What I do now is force the sentence into a shape that is hard to fudge. Not just "I don't know," but: here is what I have ruled out, here is what I am testing next, here is when I will have an answer, and here is what you should do in the meantime. The last clause is the one that matters. It converts my uncertainty into something somebody else can act on.

It also turns out that nobody on those calls was ever angry about not knowing. They were angry about finding out, two hours later, that I had not known for the whole two hours.

Certainty that is not real does not reassure anyone. It just delays the decisions that depend on the truth.

What does your team do with "I don't know yet"?
