# Instagram — eol-is-a-design-input


An end-of-life notice is a design input. 📅 Not a purchasing problem.

By the time it reaches the budget, the architecture decision has already been made for you.

The notice arrives years ahead. Four dates:

→ Last order date
→ Last ship date
→ Last day of software support
→ Last day of hardware support

All of them in a document that goes to the person who manages contracts, not the person who designs the network. ⚠️

So nothing happens. Not through negligence — the dates are distant and the platform works. The review that should have started is a conversation nobody has standing to call.

Then it becomes a budget line, and the question has changed.

It's no longer "what should this part of the network look like in three years." It's "what drops into the same slots, this fiscal year, without a redesign."

Those produce different answers. The second one is almost always the same vendor and the same topology with faster parts. ⚙️

Which may be right. But it was never decided. It was arrived at by waiting.

One more thing: the last-software-support date matters more than the hardware one, and it's the one people miss. A device that still forwards packets but can't take a security patch isn't supported in any way the business will accept.

When did you last read an EOL notice as a design document?
