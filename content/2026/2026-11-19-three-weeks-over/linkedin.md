# LinkedIn — three-weeks-over


I missed a migration estimate by three weeks. Not because the work was harder than I thought — because I had priced the work, and the project was mostly discovery.

The plan was reasonable on paper. A known number of sites, a known target design, a per-site cutover procedure proven at the first two. Multiply and add contingency. That arithmetic is how most brownfield projects get quoted, and it is wrong in a specific way.

What consumed the three weeks was not cutovers. It was finding out what was actually installed.

A handful of sites had a device that was not in the inventory. Several had a link to somewhere nobody could explain, carrying real traffic. One had a firewall rule referencing a host decommissioned years earlier, which meant the rule was either dead or load-bearing and nobody could say which. Each of those is a day or two, and none of them is cutover work. They are archaeology, one site at a time, at the worst possible moment.

So I ask two questions before quoting anything brownfield now.

First: how was this inventory produced, and when. An export from a live discovery scan last month is a different artifact from a spreadsheet maintained by hand since 2019. Both are called an inventory. Only one is evidence.

Second: when we find something undocumented, who decides what happens to it. Not escalates — decides. The schedule does not break on the discovery. It breaks on the four days the discovery waits for somebody with the authority to say "leave it" or "remove it."

Neither question is technical. Both are where the time goes.

A greenfield estimate prices the work. A brownfield estimate has to price the finding out.

What does your inventory actually say, and when was it last true?
