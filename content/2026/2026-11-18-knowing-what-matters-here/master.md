AI is good at the part we are bad at, and bad at the part we are good at.

What it is good at is reading everything. Every log line, every config, every ticket from the last three years, without getting bored on page four hundred. No human does that, and no human was ever going to.

What it is bad at is knowing what matters here. In this estate, on this night, given what this business actually does.

That distinction decides how you staff around it, and most teams get it backwards.

A model will tell you that an interface reset forty times last month. It will not tell you that the interface feeds a warehouse that only runs a day shift, so the resets land at 2am and nobody has ever noticed. It will flag a config drift on a device nobody has been able to reboot since 2021. It will not know that the reason nobody reboots it is the thing you should be fixing.

That context is not written down anywhere. It lives in the heads of people who have been there a while, and it is the actual product of experience in a specific environment.

So the useful shape is narrow. Let the model do the reading and the correlation, which is genuinely tedious and genuinely valuable. Keep the judgment with the people who know why the estate is the way it is. And write down more of that context than feels necessary, because the half of it that does get written down is the half the model can use.

The teams getting value are not the ones with the best tool. They are the ones who already had enough written down for the tool to be pointed at something real.

AI reads your environment. It does not understand your business. Staff for the difference.

What does your team know that is written down nowhere?
