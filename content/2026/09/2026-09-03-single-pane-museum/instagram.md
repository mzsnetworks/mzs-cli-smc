# Instagram — single-pane-museum

Your single pane of glass is a museum. 🏛️

Beautiful. Curated. Everything in one place, labeled, behind glass.

And like a museum, you can look at all of it and touch none of it.

I've sat in the demos. The pane is genuinely impressive — every system, one login, colour-coded.

Then the alert fires, and watch what actually happens:

→ somebody sees red on the pane
→ they leave the pane
→ they open a terminal
→ they open the ticket system to see whether anyone changed anything
→ they open the vendor portal because the device is under support
→ they message a colleague to ask what "degraded" means here

The consolidation happened at the display layer. The friction was never in the display layer. ⚙️

Dashboards show state. Work changes state.

Those are different activities, and putting the first behind a single login doesn't shorten the second at all.

So the better question isn't "can I see everything from one place." It's:

How many tools does it take to get from noticing to fixing?

That number is measurable this afternoon. Take your last three incidents and count the systems somebody had to log into and act in — not the ones that displayed something.

What I'd rather have than a bigger pane:

→ an alert that arrives with its context attached — what changed, what state, who owns it
→ a path from alert to action that doesn't cross an auth boundary three times
→ one place where work happens, even if state is displayed in several

Consolidating where you act is worth far more than consolidating where you look.

A dashboard that shows everything and changes nothing is a museum with very good lighting.

How many tools sit between your alert and your fix? 👇
