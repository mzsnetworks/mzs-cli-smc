## Thread (alternate)

1/ You can outsource the work. You can't outsource the consequence. 🧵

2/ There are good reasons to bring in a partner: after-hours coverage, specialist depth, a queue somebody else staffs at 3am. We do this work — it's real value.

3/ But something doesn't move with the contract. When the site is down, the person the business turns to still works for the business. The provider owns a scope. You own the outcome.

4/ So the question isn't what you hand over. It's what you deliberately keep.

5/ One named owner — somebody internal who can say yes or no to risk without calling a meeting. A provider can recommend an action at 2am. Only you can accept the consequence.

6/ Architecture direction — rent the hands, rent the design help, but the shape of the environment follows the business, and only you know where the business is going next year.

7/ Enough literacy to argue. If nobody on your side can challenge the report you're handed, you didn't buy a service. You bought a story.

8/ A clean exit. Where do configs live? Who holds credentials? Is the tooling yours or theirs? Answer those on a normal Tuesday, not during a divorce.

9/ You can hand over the runbook. You can hand over the pager. You can't hand over the risk. #ManagedServices #NetOps
