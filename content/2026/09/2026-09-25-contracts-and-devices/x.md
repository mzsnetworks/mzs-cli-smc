## Thread (alternate)

1/ We counted forty-two support contracts and thirty-one devices. Nobody had done anything wrong. 🧵

2/ No fraud, no incompetent purchasing. Six years of ordinary decisions and nothing in the process that ever forced the two lists to meet.

3/ How the gap opens: a device is replaced and the new one gets a new contract, while the old one runs to renewal and quietly renews. A spare is bought with coverage and sits in a cupboard. A site closes, but the support line lives in another budget.

4/ Every one of those is a reasonable act by somebody doing their job. The failure is structural — procurement tracks contracts, operations tracks devices, nothing reconciles them.

5/ And the direction everyone assumes is only half of it. Some contracts covered hardware that no longer existed.

6/ The reconciliation also found the opposite, and that's the expensive one: production devices with no coverage, because the renewal notice went to a purchasing address instead of anyone who knew what the device did.

7/ Overpaying is a line item. Finding out at 2am that your core switch has no RMA path is an outage with no known length.

8/ The fix isn't a system, it's a cadence:
• contracts from procurement, devices from the network
• match on serial number
• annually, owned by one named person
• anything unmatched gets a decision

9/ Two lists that were never wrong, and never reconciled, for six years. #NetOps #ITAM
