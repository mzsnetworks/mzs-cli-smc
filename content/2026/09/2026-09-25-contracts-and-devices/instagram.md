# Instagram — contracts-and-devices

We counted forty-two support contracts and thirty-one devices. 📋

Nobody had done anything wrong.

That's the part worth understanding. ⚙️

No fraud. No incompetent purchasing. No vendor running a scam.

Six years of ordinary decisions, and nothing in the process that ever forced the two lists to meet.

How the gap opens:

→ a device is replaced under a refresh, the new one gets a new contract, and the old contract runs to its renewal date and quietly renews
→ a spare is bought with coverage attached, sits in a cupboard, stays covered
→ a site closes and the hardware comes back, but the support line was in a different budget owned by a different person
→ a three-year term auto-renews on a device decommissioned in month twenty

Every one of those is a reasonable act by somebody doing their job.

The failure is structural. Procurement tracks contracts. Operations tracks devices. Nothing reconciles them.

And the direction everyone assumes is only half of it.

Yes — some contracts were paying for hardware that no longer existed.

The reconciliation also found the opposite, and the opposite is the expensive one: production devices with no coverage at all, because the renewal notice went to a purchasing address instead of to anyone who knew what the device did.

Overpaying is a line item.

Finding out at 2am that your core switch has no RMA path is an outage with no known length.

What fixes it isn't a system. It's a cadence:

→ pull contracts from procurement, devices from the network itself
→ match on serial number — the only field both sides share
→ do it annually, owned by one named person
→ anything unmatched in either direction gets a decision, not a note

A couple of days of work. It has never once come back clean.

When did anyone last compare the two? 👇
