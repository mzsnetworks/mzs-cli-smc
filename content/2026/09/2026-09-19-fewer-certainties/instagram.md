# Instagram — fewer-certainties

I used to think seniority was knowing more. It's mostly needing less. 🧭

Not less knowledge — less certainty.

The senior engineers I learned most from didn't have more facts loaded than anyone else in the room.

They needed fewer of them before they'd move. ⚙️

That looked like arrogance to me at first. It isn't.

It's a different skill, and it has three parts.

1️⃣ Knowing which unknowns actually block the decision

The junior instinct — mine, for years — is to gather everything, because everything feels equally load-bearing.

It isn't. On most problems there are one or two facts that change what you'd do, and a dozen that are merely interesting.

Seniority is telling those apart quickly, getting the one or two, and moving.

2️⃣ Knowing when you genuinely can't proceed

And saying so instead of producing a confident answer to fill the silence.

"I don't have enough to call this yet, and here's specifically what I need" is a senior sentence.

It's harder to say than a guess — because a guess sounds like competence and the truth sounds like hesitation.

3️⃣ Being wrong quickly, and in public

A wrong hypothesis said out loud gets corrected in ninety seconds by whoever knows better.

The same hypothesis held privately steers your next two hours, and nobody gets the chance to stop it.

The price is looking briefly unsure in front of people. That's all.

None of this is caring less. The stakes don't drop.

What drops is how much certainty you need before acting on your read — and how much time you spend collecting facts that were never going to change the answer.

Seniority isn't more certainty. It's needing less of it, and knowing exactly which certainty you still need.

What's the one fact you'd want before your next hard call? 👇
