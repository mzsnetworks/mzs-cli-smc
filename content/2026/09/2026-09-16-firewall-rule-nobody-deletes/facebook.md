# Facebook — firewall-rule-nobody-deletes

Nobody has ever been promoted for deleting a firewall rule. 🔒

Adding a rule solves a visible problem today — the ticket closes, someone says thank you. Deleting one solves nothing anyone can see, and risks breaking something nobody remembers depends on it.

One has upside. The other has only downside. Rule bases grow the way water runs downhill.

So they become archaeology: the "temporary" any/any from a migration, a vendor rule for a product retired two years ago, four near-identical rules by four different people.

The real cost isn't clutter. It's that nobody can answer what's actually allowed here — and once nobody can read it, nobody can safely change it.

Adding a rule is a ticket. Deleting one is a risk. Until that changes, your rule base only grows.

Which rule is everyone quietly scared to touch?
