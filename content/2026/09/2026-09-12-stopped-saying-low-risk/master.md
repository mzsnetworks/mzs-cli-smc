# Master — stopped-saying-low-risk

**Preset:** Professional (LinkedIn personal + Instagram @mzsnetworks)
**Date:** 2026-09-12 (Sat)
**Source idea:** ideas-2026-08-27 #7
**Thesis:** "Low risk" is a feeling compressed into two words that transfer no information. Naming what specifically breaks is both a better estimate and a better handoff.
**Stats:** none — confession/judgment post, no factcheck gate.

---

I called it low risk. It wasn't.

The change itself was small — the kind you'd describe in one line on a form. It went in, and something two hops away that I hadn't thought about stopped working, and the next ninety minutes were not low risk for anyone involved.

What stayed with me wasn't the mistake. It was realizing that "low risk" had done no work at all. It's a feeling compressed into two words. It transfers nothing. The person approving it learns neither what to watch nor when to worry, and the person writing it hasn't been forced to think.

So I stopped using the phrase. Now I write the sentence it was hiding:

"If I'm wrong, X stops working for Y people for roughly Z minutes, and here's how we get it back."

Three things changed, and only one of them was what I expected.

The obvious one: I catch more. You cannot write that sentence honestly without walking the dependency chain, and I find the surprise while writing it more often than I find it afterwards.

The second: the back-out stops being a formality. "Roll back the change" is not a plan. Naming the recovery path in the same sentence as the failure makes it concrete enough to notice when it doesn't exist.

The third one I didn't see coming — it changed who gets to say no. Someone reads it and says "not during month-end close." They're right, and they could only be right because I gave them something specific to react to. "Low risk" gives a reviewer nothing to disagree with, which feels like smooth approval and is actually just silence.

I'm not more cautious than I used to be. I ship about the same amount. What changed is that the uncertainty now lives in a sentence somebody else can read, instead of in my head where it was doing nobody any good.

"Low risk" is a feeling. "Here's what breaks" is a plan.

What's the last change you called low risk — and what would actually have broken?
