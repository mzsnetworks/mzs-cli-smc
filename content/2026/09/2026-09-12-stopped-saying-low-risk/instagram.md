# Instagram — stopped-saying-low-risk

I called it low risk. It wasn't. ⚠️

The change was small — one line on a form.

It went in. Something two hops away that I hadn't thought about stopped working.

The next ninety minutes were not low risk for anyone involved.

What stayed with me wasn't the mistake. ⚙️

It was realizing that "low risk" had done no work at all.

It's a feeling compressed into two words. It transfers nothing.

The approver learns neither what to watch nor when to worry. The writer hasn't been forced to think.

So I stopped using the phrase.

Now I write the sentence it was hiding:

"If I'm wrong, X stops working for Y people for roughly Z minutes, and here's how we get it back."

Three things changed:

→ I catch more. You can't write that sentence honestly without walking the dependency chain — and I find the surprise while writing it more often than after

→ The back-out stops being a formality. "Roll back the change" is not a plan. Naming the recovery path next to the failure makes it concrete enough to notice when it doesn't exist

→ It changed who gets to say no. Someone reads it and says "not during month-end close." They're right — and they could only be right because I gave them something specific to react to

"Low risk" gives a reviewer nothing to disagree with. That feels like smooth approval. It's just silence.

I'm not more cautious than I was. I ship about the same amount.

The uncertainty just lives in a sentence somebody else can read now, instead of in my head where it helped nobody.

"Low risk" is a feeling. "Here's what breaks" is a plan.

What's the last change you called low risk? 👇
