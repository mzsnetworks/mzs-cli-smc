# LinkedIn — who-decides


Who owns the network in your organization? Not who runs it. Who decides.

Those are different people more often than anyone expects, and the gap between them is where projects go to wait.

Running it is easy to locate. There is a team, a rota, an escalation path, a mailbox. Ask who fixes it at 2am and you get a name in seconds.

Deciding is harder. Who approves a standard that constrains what a business unit can buy? Who says the refresh happens this year rather than next? Who settles it when security wants segmentation and operations wants the change window shorter? Those answers tend to arrive as "it depends," which is usually true and never useful.

What we see in practice is that the network is owned operationally by a team with no budget authority, and owned financially by somebody who has never been asked a technical question about it. Neither of them can make the decision alone. Nobody has told them that.

So the standard gets drafted and never ratified. The refresh slips a year at a time, each time for a defensible reason. The segmentation project has three sponsors and no owner, which is the same as none.

The tell is simple. If a decision about the network has been open for more than a quarter and nobody can say who is supposed to close it, the problem is not the decision. It is that ownership was never assigned, only assumed.

This is not solved with a diagram or a RACI chart that nobody reads. It is solved by naming one person who can say yes, and telling them.

A network without an owner does not stop working. It stops changing, which looks the same for a while.

Who in your organization can approve a network standard without asking anyone?
