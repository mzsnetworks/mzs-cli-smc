# LinkedIn — without-asking-you


What would you actually let an AI change without asking you first?

Ask that in a room of engineers and the honest answer is almost always nothing. Not one interface. Not a route. Not a queue depth on a device that serves eleven people. Nothing.

That answer is the real state of AIOps adoption, and it is more useful than any maturity model.

The platforms are sold on detection, and detection has genuinely gotten good. Correlating thousands of events into one probable cause, spotting the pattern hours before the pager, suggesting the specific action that fixes it. I have watched tools do all of that and be right.

Then a human reads the suggestion and types it in. Every time. Which means the entire value delivered is "noticed faster," and the industry keeps describing that as autonomous operations.

The interesting part is that the reluctance is not about accuracy. Nobody I have asked says the recommendation is usually wrong. What they say, in different words, is: I cannot explain that change to anyone afterwards, and I cannot get it back cleanly if it is wrong.

Which reframes the problem. The blocker on autonomous action is not model quality. It is that the surrounding system has no undo and no account of itself. If a change can be reversed in thirty seconds, and the record of what changed and why is written where a human will find it, the trust question gets much smaller. If neither is true, no amount of model confidence should move you.

So the work is unglamorous, and it is not AI work. Make changes reversible, make them legible, and what to delegate becomes answerable one action at a time.

Autonomy is not something a platform grants you. It is something your rollback path earns.

What is the first thing you would hand over, and what would have to be true first?
