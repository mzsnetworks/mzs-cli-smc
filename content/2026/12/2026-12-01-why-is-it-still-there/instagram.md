# Instagram — why-is-it-still-there


What's the oldest device in your production path? 🗄️

And why is it still there?

Most people answer that in seconds. A specific box, in a specific rack. That it came to mind that fast says something.

The second part is where it gets interesting — the answer is almost never technical. ⚠️

It isn't there because it's the right tool. It's there because it works, and removing it would require somebody to say what depends on it — and the person who knew that left in 2021.

I've stopped treating this as a lifecycle problem. Lifecycle says: it's old, replace it. That loses every time — the device isn't causing a problem today, and the replacement has a cost today. ⚙️

The better question is dependency. Not "how old is it" but "what happens if it stops, and who finds out first."

If you can't answer that, the age isn't the risk. Not knowing is.

So the exercise is small: spend an hour on what actually traverses it. Not the diagram — the device, the flow data, the two people most likely to shrug.

One of two things comes out:

→ It carries almost nothing, and can go next quarter
→ It's load-bearing in a way written down nowhere

Both are worth the hour. The current state is neither answer.

Old isn't the problem. Old and unexamined is.

What's yours, and who would have to agree to remove it?
