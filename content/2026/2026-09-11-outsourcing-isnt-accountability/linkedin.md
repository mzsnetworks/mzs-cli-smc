# LinkedIn — outsourcing-isnt-accountability


You can outsource the work. You can't outsource the consequence.

There are good reasons to bring in a partner. After-hours coverage. Specialist depth. A queue somebody else staffs at 3am. Hands you don't have to hire. We do this work — it's real value.

But something doesn't move with the contract. When the site is down, the person the business turns to still works for the business. The provider owns a scope. You own the outcome.

Which makes outsourcing the wrong question. The question is what you deliberately keep.

Four things we tell clients not to give away.

**One named owner.** Somebody internal who can say yes or no to risk without calling a meeting. A provider can recommend an action at 2am. Only you can accept the consequence of it.

**Architecture direction.** Rent the hands, rent the design help — but the shape of the environment follows the business, and only you know where the business is going next year.

**Enough literacy to argue.** If nobody on your side can challenge the report you're handed, you didn't buy a service. You bought a story. One engineer who can read a config and ask an awkward question is worth more than another tier of support.

**A clean exit.** Where do the configs live? Who holds the credentials? Is the tooling yours or theirs? You want those answers on a normal Tuesday, not during a divorce.

Here's the uncomfortable version, and we'll say it about ourselves: the best partnerships we're in are the ones where the client can argue with us. The ones that go badly are the ones where nobody internally can tell whether the answer was any good.

Outsourcing done well buys capacity and a second opinion. Done badly it buys distance from your own environment — and distance is the last thing you want when it breaks.

You can hand over the runbook. You can hand over the pager. You can't hand over the risk.

If your provider went dark on Monday, how long before you knew what to do?
