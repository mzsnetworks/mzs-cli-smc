# Instagram — orchestration-decision-making

Orchestration isn't automation at scale. It's decision-making at scale. 🧠

The pitch: forty scripts, three teams, buy the orchestrator, now you have a platform.

Then the tool arrives and the real work is questions nobody wrote down:

→ what runs first
→ what blocks what
→ what can run in parallel without racing for the same device
→ step six fails after step five changed state — roll back, hold, or wake someone up?
→ who decides that at 2am — the tool, or a person?

Those are decisions. Scripts don't make decisions. Engineers made them in their heads, one change at a time — a human was the orchestrator. ⚙️

The tool doesn't discover your dependency graph. It executes the one you hand it. Hand it a wrong graph and you've bought a faster way to be wrong, at scale.

The whiteboard test: can't draw the order, the blockers and the rollback path? Not ready to buy the tool that automates it. 📋

Scripts execute. Orchestrators decide.

Where does your dependency graph live — a diagram, or a person? 👇
