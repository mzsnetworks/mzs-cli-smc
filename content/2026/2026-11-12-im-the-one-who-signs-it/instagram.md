# Instagram — im-the-one-who-signs-it


I use AI to write configs most days. 🤖 And I still read every line.

Not because it's usually wrong. Because I'm the one who signs it.

That distinction matters more than the accuracy argument — which is the one everybody has instead. ⚠️

The accuracy argument goes in circles. Somebody says the model gets it wrong. Somebody else says so do people. Both are right in a way that settles nothing.

The real question isn't how often it's wrong. It's who is accountable when it is.

When a change goes into production under my name, I answer for it at the review. Not the tool. Not the vendor of the tool.

And I can't answer for something I didn't read — which means reading it isn't a quality step I can optimize away. It's the thing that makes the output mine. ⚙️

In practice it's less dramatic than it sounds:

→ The model drafts. Genuinely faster than typing
→ I read it like a colleague's change: what does it do, what does it assume, what does it touch that I didn't ask about

That last one catches the most. Models are helpful, and helpful sometimes means adding a line you didn't request because it usually goes with the thing you did.

"The model wrote it" has never been an acceptable answer to "why is this in production."

Would you sign a change you hadn't read?
