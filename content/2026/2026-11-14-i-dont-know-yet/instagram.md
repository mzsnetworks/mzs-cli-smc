# Instagram — i-dont-know-yet


Have you ever been unable to say "I don't know yet" during an incident? 🤐

I have. Not because anyone forbade it.

Because of who was on the call, and because forty minutes in it felt like admitting I'd wasted forty minutes.

So I said something else. Something shaped like progress:

"We're seeing some irregularity on the WAN side, still narrowing it down."

All true. All useless. ⚠️ And it bought me fifteen more minutes of not being asked anything.

What that sentence actually did was cost the organization fifteen minutes.

Because people make decisions off what you say:

→ "Still narrowing it down" means hold. Stay on the call. Don't start the workaround
→ "I don't know yet, and I won't for an hour" means start the manual process, tell the client, call the second team

Those are useful decisions. They were available the whole time. ⚙️

The admission isn't the expensive part. The vagueness is.

What I do now: what I've ruled out, what I'm testing next, when I'll have an answer, and what you should do meanwhile.

That last clause is the one that matters. It turns my uncertainty into something somebody else can act on.

Nobody was ever angry about not knowing. They were angry about finding out two hours later that I hadn't known the whole time.

What does your team do with "I don't know yet"?
