# Instagram — uptime-is-lagging

Uptime is a lagging indicator. It tells you what survived — not what's about to break. ⏱️

We report it because it's the easiest number in the building to count.

Four nines looks like a job well done. Right up until the quarter it isn't — and nobody saw it coming, because the metric was never built to warn anyone.

The pair worth watching instead:

→ how fast the environment changes
→ how much review those changes actually get

Velocity itself isn't the risk. Per DORA's State of DevOps research, the fastest-moving teams are also the most stable — throughput and stability rise together, they aren't a trade.

The risk is velocity outrunning review discipline. ⚖️

That gap is visible long before an outage:

→ the "standard change" list keeps growing, because exceptions are slow
→ approvals come back in under a minute — approvals, not readings
→ rollback is a paragraph in the ticket nobody has executed
→ the person who understands the change is the person approving it

Every one of those is measurable this week. None of them need an incident first.

Uptime tells you what survived. Change velocity tells you what's coming. Review discipline decides which one you get.

When did anyone last check the ratio? 👇
