"Rip and replace" is a fantasy budget line.

The slide always reads the same: decommission the old network, stand up the new one, cut over on a weekend. Clean boxes, clean arrows, one bold move.

We've read that slide many times. We've never watched it happen.

Because the old network doesn't want to die. It's carrying production traffic right now — sessions nobody can drop, an application nobody dares restart, a payment flow that quietly runs through the "legacy" switch on the decommission list. Dependencies nobody documented, discovered the way they always are: by breaking.

There is no weekend long enough for all of that. The riskiest phrase in any modernization plan is "and then we cut over."

Real modernization happens live. One migration at a time — a segment, a service, a site — on a network that never stops carrying traffic. Run the new alongside the old. Move one thing. Verify it. Move the next. The old gear leaves last, not first.

That's slower on the slide and faster in reality, because none of the moves are bets. Each one is small, verified, and reversible — and reversible is the whole game in brownfield.

Rip-and-replace isn't bold. It's a way to skip the real work: understanding what's actually running. The parallel-run plan looks timid and requires total knowledge of your environment. That's why it works.

You don't replace a production network. You migrate it — piece by piece, live — until nothing old remains and nobody noticed the surgery.

If the plan needs downtime nobody will approve, it isn't a plan. It's a wish.

How many rip-and-replace projects have you seen finish on the original slide?
