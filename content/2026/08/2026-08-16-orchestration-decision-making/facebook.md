# Facebook — orchestration-decision-making

Orchestration isn't automation at scale. It's decision-making at scale.

Buy the orchestrator and you'll have a platform, the pitch goes. Then the tool arrives and the real work is questions nobody wrote down: what runs first, what blocks what, what happens when step six fails after step five already changed state.

Scripts don't make those decisions — and the orchestrator doesn't discover them. It executes the dependency graph you hand it. Hand it a wrong one and you've bought a faster way to be wrong, at scale.

The whiteboard test: if your team can't draw the graph — order, blockers, rollback path — you're not ready to buy the tool that automates it.

Scripts execute. Orchestrators decide.

Where does your dependency graph live — in a diagram, or in a person?
