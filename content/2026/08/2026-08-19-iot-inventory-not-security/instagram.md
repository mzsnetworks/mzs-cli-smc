# Instagram — iot-inventory-not-security

Your IoT problem isn't security. It's inventory. 🔍

The budget points at segmentation, NAC, firewalls. Then someone asks the first real question — what's actually on the network? — and the room goes quiet.

Not what the spreadsheet says. What's drawing an IP right now.

Why nobody knows:
→ facilities buys the cameras
→ clinical buys the pumps
→ marketing buys the lobby displays
→ a pilot ends, the team disbands, the devices stay powered on for years

IoT never arrives through the network team. The MAC address table is the most honest document in the building. 📋

A policy needs a subject. "Least privilege for IoT" isn't a policy until you can say which devices, doing what, talking to whom. Write the rule before the count and you get one that permits everything — or one that breaks something load-bearing at 3am. ⚠️

The order that works is boring:
→ enumerate what's there
→ identify what each thing is
→ classify by behavior
→ then write policy per class

Enumeration finds things nobody wants to own. That's not a reason to skip it — it's the value of it. Those devices were risks the whole time. Just unlisted.

Count first. Segment second.

How many devices are on your network right now — within ten? 👇
