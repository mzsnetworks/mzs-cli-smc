## Single (publish this)

Your IoT problem isn't security. It's inventory.

"Least privilege for IoT" isn't a policy until you can say which devices, doing what, talking to whom. You can't write policy for a device you can't name.

Count first. Segment second.

## Thread (alternate)

1/ Your IoT problem isn't security. It's inventory.

2/ The budget points at segmentation, NAC, firewalls. Then someone asks what's actually on the network, and the room goes quiet.

3/ IoT never arrives through the network team. Facilities buys the cameras. Clinical buys the pumps. A pilot ends, the team disbands, the devices stay powered on for years.

4/ The MAC address table is the most honest document in the building. It doesn't care what the spreadsheet claims.

5/ A policy needs a subject. Write the rule before the count and you get one that permits everything — or one that breaks something load-bearing at 3am.

6/ The order that works is boring: enumerate, identify, classify by behavior, then policy per class.

7/ You can't write policy for a device you can't name. Count first. Segment second. #IoT
