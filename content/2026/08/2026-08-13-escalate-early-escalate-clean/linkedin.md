# LinkedIn — escalate-early-escalate-clean

Escalate early, escalate clean.

Early in my career I held on to burning issues way too long. Escalating felt like admitting I couldn't do my job. So I'd keep digging while the outage clock ran — and the clock doesn't care about pride.

Here's what I eventually understood: the junior move is holding the issue to prove you can solve it alone. The senior move is a handoff so clean the next person starts where you stopped, not where you started.

The evidence packet is three lines:

What changed. Last known-good, first known-bad, and everything touched in between — config pushes, patches, physical work, "unrelated" tickets.

What you ruled out. With the outputs that rule it out — so nobody re-walks your path. "It's not physical, interface counters are clean" beats "I checked some stuff."

What you need. A decision, an access you don't have, a vendor case, a second pair of eyes. Name it. "Help" is not a request; "I need someone who can read this stack trace" is.

And the timing rule: escalate when your learning rate drops, not when you run out of ideas. The moment you're re-running the same three commands hoping for different output, you've stopped troubleshooting. You're coping.

Escalation isn't transferring failure. It's transferring context. The engineers I trust most escalate earlier than anyone — and their handoffs read like case files.

Held issues age into outages. Shared issues age into fixes.

What's your escalation trigger — a timer, or a gut feeling?
