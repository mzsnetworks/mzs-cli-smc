# LinkedIn — load-bearing-workaround

Every network has a "temporary" workaround old enough to attend school.

Here's how you know yours became load-bearing infrastructure:

1. It has a nickname. The moment the team names it, it's a system.

2. New hires get taught it. It's not documented — but it's in onboarding.

3. Its alert is permanently suppressed. Monitoring learned to look away.

4. Something downstream depends on its quirks. "Fixing" it breaks a thing you can't explain.

5. Nobody remembers the problem it solved. Only that removing it is scary.

6. It survived a redesign. The architecture changed; the workaround got migrated with care.

None of these happened by decision. That's the mechanism: workarounds become load-bearing through time, not approval. Every day nobody removes it is a sign-off nobody signed.

Operationally, you have two honest options. Promote it — document it, monitor it, support it like the infrastructure it already is. Or schedule its removal — a date, an owner, a plan.

The one thing you can't keep doing is calling it temporary.

Temporary is a claim about intent. Load-bearing is a fact about dependency. The difference is whether removal has a date.
